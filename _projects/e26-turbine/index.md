**Project Type** - UC Berkeley
**Collaborators** - Mia Galatis, Benjamin Kao, Doran Chang, Eduardo Reyes Garcia

The E26 wind turbine project is a competition among students to see who can design a wind turbine that outputs the most power while using the least amount of material to manufacture. All teams were given the same generator and placed under the same “wind” conditions (generated by a fan) and power generation was recorded. The structure was then tested by hanging weights where the motor sat until the structure failed.

## Design and CAD
### **Rotor Design**
Factors such as blade length, chord length, angle of attack, twist, and the number of blades were considered during the design process. The goal was to maximize the force generated *by* the blade while minimizing force exerted *on* the blade.

The final rotor design had 3 blades, a 15° angle of attack, 12° angle of twist, and used a DU 97-W-300 blade profile (pictured below).

{% include image-gallery.html images="rotor-cad.png" height="400" %}
{% include image-gallery.html images="profile.png" height="400" %}

The rotor was 3D printed out of clear resin on a Formlabs SLA printer for precise geometry and a smooth finish to reduce friction.

### **Structure Design**
The tower was designed to minimize material use without sacrificing structural integrity. The tower was 3D printed out of PLA. There were three parts: the base, the middle pillar, and the motor cradle. Pins allowed them to slot together and they were glued in place for additional rigidity. ## FEA
Finite Element Analysis was done within SOLIDWORKS to test the strength of the turbine structure. The bottom of the base was fixed and a force of 9.81 N (~1 kg) was applied to where the blade would push on the structure.

{% include image-gallery.html images="stress.jpg, Strain.jpg, Displacement.jpg" height="400" caption="Stress, strain, and displacement plots from FEA." %}

These results gave us confidence our design would hold up under load. 

## Testing and Results
Once the tower was assembled and the motor in place, the wind turbine was fasted down in front of a fan, which was turned on and simulated 25 mph winds. A generator was hooked up to the motor and the power output was recorded, along with the rotation speed, as measured by a tachometer.
